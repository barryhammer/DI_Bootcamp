Biography

Stanley Kubrick (July 26, 1928 – March 7, 1999) was an American film director, screenwriter, producer, and photographer whose exacting craft and fearless imagination left an enduring mark on world cinema.

Born in New York City and raised in the Bronx, Kubrick developed an early passion for photography. At just 17, he began working as a staff photographer for Look magazine. The experience sharpened his eye for composition, atmosphere, and the telling detail—qualities that would become hallmarks of his films.

He moved into filmmaking in the early 1950s, making documentary shorts before directing his first feature, Fear and Desire (1953). Across a career spanning nearly five decades, he moved confidently between war films, satire, science fiction, historical drama, horror, and psychological mystery.

Known for meticulous research, striking visual design, and an uncompromising approach to production, Kubrick often worked across directing, writing, editing, and producing. He settled in England in the 1960s and continued making films there. His final feature, Eyes Wide Shut, was released in 1999, after his death.
